Dear Mr. Walker,

I'm a Bay Area parent and have been building a small, browser-based tool to address a specific advising challenge: helping students and families understand what college pathways are structurally realistic at a given GPA and point in time — before expectations anchor on outcomes that require circumstances the student's profile doesn't support.

In high-achieving districts like Acalanes, the challenge isn't floor-level access — it's calibrating realistic ambition. The tool surfaces what is structurally viable, where effort still changes outcomes, and where the window has already closed. It doesn't rank campuses or predict outcomes. It creates a shared, defensible baseline for counselors to start from.

I'm in early conversations with several Bay Area districts and would value 20 minutes of your reaction — specifically whether this kind of structural framing would be useful, redundant, or unwelcome in your context.
