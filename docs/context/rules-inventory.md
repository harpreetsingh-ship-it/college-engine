# Master Rule Inventory

**Version:** v0.9 (canonical)
**Status:** Source of truth for all rule families

> No UX language. No examples. No persuasion. Just rules.

---

## A. Time & Irreversibility Rules

**A1. Grade-Based Closure Rules**
- 9th → all pathways open
- End of 10th → early closure begins
- End of 11th → most elite pathways lock
- 12th (Oct+) → outcomes largely fixed

**A2. Freshman-Year Exclusion Rule**
- UC/CSU do not count 9th-grade GPA
- OOS/private do

**A3. Senior-Year Illusion Rule**
- 12th-grade GPA changes rarely affect admissions
- Senior rigor affects rescind risk only

---

## B. GPA Band Rules (Primary Gates)

**B1. Band A (≥3.8)**
- Top-tier UC viable
- Elite privates viable
- Testing acts as amplifier
- ECs are mandatory tie-breakers

**B2. Band B (3.5–3.8)**
- Mid-tier UCs viable
- OOS publics viable (major-dependent)
- Internships + EC spikes matter
- CC optional, not required

**B3. Band C (3.2–3.5)**
- Low/mid UCs limited
- CSU preferred
- CC→UC strongly recommended
- TAG / TAP become primary levers

**B4. Band D (2.8–3.2)**
- CSU only
- CC coursework prioritized
- ECs and testing suppressed

**B5. Band E (<2.8)**
- CC or limited CSU
- Academic stabilization only
- All signaling suppressed

## C. Community College / Transfer Rules

**C1. Dual Enrollment Benefit Rule**
- Highest ROI in GPA bands 3.2–3.5

**C2. TAG Rule**
- 60 units + ~3.2 CC GPA → guaranteed mid-tier UC

**C3. TAP Rule**
- Honors pathways materially increase UC odds
- UCLA TAP via LPC is exemplar

**C4. CC Performance Permanence Rule**
- CC grades are permanent
- Cs/Ds damage long-term outcomes

**C5. Aligned CC Signal Exception (Non-Transfer Use)**
In non-impacted majors, aligned CC coursework completed with A grades may function as a signal of academic seriousness even when the student does not intend to transfer via CC. This exception does not apply to random or misaligned coursework and does not override GPA band limits.

## L. Major Realignment Rules

**L1. Structural Mismatch Detection Rule**
If the student's GPA band and grade concentration are structurally incompatible with the academic demands of the intended major, the engine must flag a major–profile mismatch.

**L2. Realign or Repair Mandate**
When a mismatch is detected, the engine must force one of two paths:
- Repair: targeted academic remediation that preserves the major, or
- Realign: shift to a related major or pathway with higher probability of success.

**L3. Pathway vs Major Distinction Rule**
For STEM profiles with strong technical ability but collateral academic damage, prefer pathway realignment (e.g., CSU, CC→UC) over major abandonment.

**L4. Non-Impacted Major Flexibility Rule**
For non-impacted majors (e.g., humanities/social sciences), selective programs may tolerate isolated quantitative weakness if the overall academic and narrative profile is coherent.

## M. Campus Personality Layer

**M1. Purpose**
The campus personality layer modifies campus-level viability and messaging after global GPA/major gates are applied. It does not change GPA bands or create guarantees.

**M2. UC Berkeley — Spike-Sensitive, Floor-Guarded**
- Rewards sharp intellectual identity, leadership, and recognition
- More tolerant of early academic anomalies (≤10th grade) if repaired
- Less tolerant of late anomalies (11th grade), even for non-quant majors
- Prefers depth over smoothness; rejects many "safe" profiles
- May admit applicants rejected by other UCs

**M3. UCLA — Polish and Consistency Optimized**
- Prefers smooth, consistent academic records
- Less tolerant of visible anomalies
- Values coherent, well-presented narratives over sharp spikes

**M4. UCSD / UCI / UCD — Predictability and Smoothing**
- Optimize for academic reliability and low variance
- Penalize Ds/Fs even if repaired
- Often reject spiky or uneven profiles Berkeley may accept

**M5. UCSB — Balanced Excellence**
- Values strong academics with coherence
- Less spike-driven than Berkeley, less smoothing-focused than UCSD/UCI

**M6. UCSC / UCR — Access and Context**
- More receptive to recovery stories and realistic preparation
- Strong fit and upward trend matter more than polish

**M7. UCM — Access**
- Access UC campus
- Viable (direct) for Band C even if trend is flat
- Conditional viable (direct) for Band D only if trend is improving and major is non-impacted

**M8. Asymmetric Outcome Rule**
A student may be admitted to a more selective UC while being rejected by a less selective one due to differing campus selection philosophies. This is normal and expected, not an error.

## Campus Personality Rules (CP Series)

**CP1 — UC Campus Floors Are Not Symmetric**
UC campuses have different effective GPA floors, even within the "lower UC" set. Treat UCM separately from UCR/UCSC.

**CP2 — UCM is an Access UC (Band C Friendly)**
- UCM middle 25–75: 3.42–4.04; ~20% of admits in 3.25–3.49
- In Band C (3.20–3.49): UCM appears as Viable (Direct) even with flat trend
- In Band D (2.80–3.19): UCM appears only as Conditional if trend is improving and major is non-impacted

**CP3 — UCR is Floor-Guarded (~3.50+)**
- UCR middle 25–75: 3.65–4.16; only ~6% in 3.25–3.49
- In Band C: do not show UCR as viable by default
- UCR only appears in Band C via rare-signal exception (not in baseline logic)

**CP4 — UCSC is Floor-Guarded (Stricter than UCR)**
- UCSC middle 25–75: 3.83–4.20; only ~4.8% in 3.25–3.49
- Same rule as UCR, but stricter: do not show UCSC in Band C unless explicit rare exception is encoded

**CP5 — Labeling Rule (No "tiers" language in outputs)**
Use campus-specific labels instead of tier language:
- "UC Merced (UCM) — access UC option"
- "UC Riverside (UCR) — floor-guarded (rare below ~3.5)"
- "UC Santa Cruz (UCSC) — floor-guarded (rare below ~3.5)"
