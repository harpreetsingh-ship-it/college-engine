# 15-Minute Demo Script

**Audience:** District counseling directors / student services leads
**Format:** Zoom screen share
**Goal:** Institutional fit reaction — not adoption commitment

---

## Minute 0–2 — Opening Frame

> "Thank you for taking the time today. I want to be very clear upfront about what this is and what it is not.
>
> This is not an admissions predictor.
> It is not a replacement for counselor judgment.
> It is not a decision engine.
>
> It's a structural guardrail tool.
>
> I built this after noticing a recurring ambiguity in high school advising conversations. Families often interpret rigor, GPA, and pathway options very differently. Counselors are working within clear structural constraints — UC/CSU eligibility bands, GPA realities, transfer mechanics — but those constraints aren't always externalized in a fast, consistent way.
>
> This tool attempts to externalize those structural constraints in under two minutes."

*Pause.*

---

## Minute 2–5 — The Structural Pattern

> "What I've observed across districts — and also as a parent — is that three friction points tend to show up:
>
> First, GPA interpretation varies depending on how it's explained in conversation.
>
> Second, over-rigor decisions sometimes erode GPA without families fully understanding downstream pathway implications.
>
> Third, community college transfer pathways are often introduced reactively, rather than proactively framed as structured, viable options.
>
> This tool does not attempt to solve admissions.
>
> It simply makes pathway guardrails visible early."

*Keep tone analytical. Not emotional.*

---

## Minute 5–10 — Live Walkthrough

*Now screen share.*

> "What I'm going to show you takes under two minutes."

Enter GPA.

> "You'll notice that based purely on GPA banding, the engine categorizes the student into a structural pathway band."

Click.

> "What this does is externalize typical UC/CSU guardrails aligned with that GPA range. It doesn't guarantee anything. It doesn't predict outcomes. It simply reflects structural admissions realities."

Scroll.

> "The language is intentionally conservative. It preserves counselor discretion. It does not rank students. It does not store data. It does not replace conversation."

*Pause.*

> "The value here is consistency. **If two counselors have this tool open, they are anchored to the same structural framing.**"

*That sentence matters.*

---

## Minute 10–12 — Institutional Value

> "From an institutional perspective, the value is alignment and ambiguity reduction.
>
> It supports:
>
> – Consistent messaging across counseling teams
> – Clear documentation of structural guidance
> – Faster pathway clarity for families
> – Reduced confusion in high-stakes conversations
>
> It can also support equity of messaging across campuses by ensuring that structural guardrails are externalized consistently."

*Keep "equity" measured. No moral tone.*

---

## Minute 12–14 — Boundaries & Risk Containment

> "I want to clearly state what this tool does not do.
>
> It does not predict admission outcomes.
> It does not override counselor judgment.
> It does not store student data.
> It does not create student records.
> It does not evaluate extracurriculars or personal narratives.
>
> It simply reflects GPA-based structural pathway guardrails."

*This is where trust solidifies.*

---

## Minute 14–15 — Close

> "I'm not proposing adoption today. I'm looking for feedback on institutional fit.
>
> Would something like this meaningfully support counseling consistency in your district?
> Or would it introduce complexity you don't need?"
